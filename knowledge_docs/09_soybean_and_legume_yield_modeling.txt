Soybean (Glycine max) is a premier global oilseed and high-protein crop. Yield modeling in soybean centers on pod production per plant, seeds per pod, and individual 100-seed test weight:

1. Yield Determinants in Soybean:
- Yield equation: Total Yield = (Plants / ha) × (Pods / plant) × (Seeds / pod) × (Seed weight).
- Pod number per plant is the single most variable yield component, accounting for 70% of seasonal yield variance. Pod abortion occurs rapidly if moisture or nitrogen stress hits between growth stages R3 (beginning pod) and R5 (beginning seed).

2. Typical National Yield Ranges:
- USA / Brazil / Argentina: 2.7 to 3.5 tonnes/ha (27,000 to 35,000 hg/ha).
- India (Central Rainfed Plateau): 1.0 to 1.5 tonnes/ha (10,000 to 15,000 hg/ha).
- China: 1.8 to 2.2 tonnes/ha (18,000 to 22,000 hg/ha).

3. Sensitivity to High Daytime Temperatures:
Soybean photosynthetic assimilation drops sharply when ambient temperatures exceed 32°C. Sustained daytime highs of 36°C during the flowering stage cause excessive flower abscission, leading to irreversible yield reductions of 20-35%.
