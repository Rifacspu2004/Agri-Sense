Cereal crops (Maize, Wheat, Paddy Rice) provide over 50% of direct human caloric intake. Global yield benchmarks vary widely based on irrigation, input technology, and climate:

1. Maize (Zea mays) Yield Benchmarks:
- Global Average Yield: 5.8 tonnes/ha (58,000 hg/ha).
- High-Technology Irrigated/Temperate Yields (USA Corn Belt, France, New Zealand): 9.5 to 13.0 tonnes/ha (95,000 to 130,000 hg/ha).
- Sub-Saharan African & Rainfed Tropical Averages: 1.8 to 2.5 tonnes/ha (18,000 to 25,000 hg/ha).
- Yield Driver: Maize is extremely sensitive to moisture stress in the 2-week window spanning silking and tasseling; drought during this window can slash predicted yields by 40-60%.

2. Wheat (Triticum aestivum) Yield Benchmarks:
- Global Average Yield: 3.5 tonnes/ha (35,000 hg/ha).
- High-Yield Western Europe (UK, Germany, France): 7.5 to 9.0 tonnes/ha (75,000 to 90,000 hg/ha).
- Indo-Gangetic Plains (India, Pakistan): 3.2 to 4.5 tonnes/ha (32,000 to 45,000 hg/ha).
- Australian & Semi-Arid Rainfed: 1.8 to 2.8 tonnes/ha (18,000 to 28,000 hg/ha).
- Yield Driver: Yield is determined by number of grains per spike and individual grain weight. Heat stress (>32°C during anthesis) triggers premature senescence, reducing yields by 4-5% per degree rise.

3. Rice, Paddy (Oryza sativa) Yield Benchmarks:
- Global Average Yield: 4.6 tonnes/ha (46,000 hg/ha).
- High Productivity (Australia, Egypt, Japan, USA): 7.0 to 10.0 tonnes/ha (70,000 to 100,000 hg/ha).
- South & Southeast Asia (India, Indonesia, Vietnam): 3.0 to 5.5 tonnes/ha (30,000 to 55,000 hg/ha).
- Yield Driver: Solar radiation during panicle initiation and consistent water availability during booting and heading phases.
