Annual precipitation is one of the most critical numerical inputs in tabular yield prediction models. However, the correlation between rainfall and yield is non-linear and crop-dependent:

1. Non-Linear Yield Response Curves:
- In low-rainfall bands (< 400 mm/year): Yield follows a steep upward trajectory as moisture availability relieves severe stomatal closure and vegetative stunting.
- Optimal rainfall window (600 - 1500 mm/year for most cereals and pulses): Yield achieves maximum efficiency per millimeter of transpiration.
- Excessive rainfall (> 2200 mm/year): The curve flattens and drops due to soil waterlogging, root hypoxia (oxygen depletion), increased fungal disease pressure, and nutrient leaching (especially nitrates and sulfur).

2. Critical Phenological Water Stress Windows:
- Maize: Tasseling and silking (Yield penalty: 4% to 8% per day of severe moisture stress).
- Wheat: Crown root initiation (21 DAS) and flowering/grain fill (Yield penalty: 3% to 5% per day).
- Rice: Panicle initiation to flowering (Moisture deficit causes spikelet sterility exceeding 70%).
- Soybeans: Pod filling (R3 to R5 stages; water deficit causes flower shedding and flat empty pods).

3. Irrigation Compensation:
In regions with low natural precipitation (e.g., Egypt at ~51 mm/yr, Pakistan at ~494 mm/yr), crop yields remain high (e.g., Egyptian maize at ~7.9 tonnes/ha) because predictive models implicitly account for intensive canal and Nile river basin irrigation systems.
