The 'Yield Gap' is defined as the difference between the theoretical yield potential (Yp) achieved under disease-free, fully irrigated conditions and the actual average farm yield (Ya) harvested by growers in a specific region.

1. Regional Variations in Actual vs Potential Yields:
- North America & Western Europe:
  - Exploitable yield gap is narrow (typically only 15% - 25%).
  - Actual yields hover close to theoretical potential due to high genetic purity of F1 hybrids, GPS-guided variable rate fertilizer application, and comprehensive crop protection.
- South Asia (India, Pakistan, Bangladesh):
  - Yield gap stands at 30% - 50%.
  - Factors limiting actual yields include suboptimal nitrogen-phosphorus-potassium balance, delayed wheat sowing due to late paddy harvesting, and episodic monsoon irregularities.
- Sub-Saharan Africa:
  - Yield gap is wide (60% - 75%).
  - Actual cereal yields hover around 1.5 - 2.5 t/ha against agro-ecological potentials of 6.0 - 8.0 t/ha.
  - Primary constraints: Phosphorus fixation in weathered oxisols, low fertilizer access (<20 kg nutrients/ha compared to 150+ kg in Europe), and reliance on unselected landrace seeds.

2. Significance for Yield Prediction Algorithms:
When training predictive machine learning models across countries (e.g. Area feature), the model learns these baseline institutional, genetic, and infrastructural constants. A model predicting maize yield in the USA will output ~9.5 t/ha, whereas for the identical rainfall and temperature in Kenya it might predict ~2.2 t/ha, reflecting the regional yield ceiling.
