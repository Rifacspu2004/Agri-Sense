Grain Sorghum (Sorghum bicolor) functions as an essential drought-hardy staple across dryland agro-ecologies where maize frequently suffers catastrophic yield failure.

1. Agronomic Yield Boundaries:
- Commercial High-Input (USA, Australia): 4.0 to 6.5 tonnes/ha (40,000 to 65,000 hg/ha).
- Rainfed Semi-Arid Tropics (India, Nigeria, Sudan): 0.9 to 1.8 tonnes/ha (9,000 to 18,000 hg/ha).

2. Physiological Adaptations Dictating Yield Stability:
- C4 Photosynthetic Efficiency: Exhibits higher water-use efficiency (transpiration ratio: 300-350 kg water per kg dry matter compared to 500-600 kg in C3 wheat).
- Osmotic Adjustment & Leaf Rolling: During severe dry spells, sorghum halts expansion and rolls leaves to curtail transpiration, entering a semi-dormant state, resuming development when rains recur.
- Stomatal Conductance: Stomata close promptly at lower leaf water potentials without causing cellular membrane destruction.

3. Yield Prediction in Erratic Monsoon Zones:
In supervised regression pipelines, sorghum demonstrates the lowest variance to rainfall deviations between 400 mm and 800 mm compared to all other cereals.
