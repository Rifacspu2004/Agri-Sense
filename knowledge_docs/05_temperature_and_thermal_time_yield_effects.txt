Temperature dictates the metabolic rate of plants, leaf appearance rates, and total duration of the crop growth cycle. In machine learning yield models, average temperature serves as a proxy for thermal energy and heat stress risk.

1. Thermal Acceleration and Growth Duration:
- Higher temperatures accelerate crop development (phenology), meaning the crop reaches maturity faster.
- Yield Penalty of Shortened Growth: A shorter vegetative and grain-filling duration limits the total cumulative solar radiation intercepted by the canopy. For wheat, every 1°C increase above the optimum decreases grain duration by 2.8 days, cutting final grain weight by 4-6%.

2. Critical Cardinal Temperature Thresholds:
- Cool Season Crops (Wheat, Barley, Potatoes):
  - Base Temperature (Tb): 4°C to 5°C.
  - Optimum Range: 15°C to 22°C.
  - Maximum Threshold: > 30°C causes pollen sterility and sharp yield degradation.
- Warm Season Crops (Maize, Rice, Sorghum, Soybean):
  - Base Temperature (Tb): 10°C.
  - Optimum Range: 24°C to 30°C.
  - Maximum Threshold: > 35°C during flowering causes silk desiccation in maize and floret sterility in rice.

3. Night Temperature Effects:
Elevated night temperatures accelerate dark respiration, consuming stored assimilates and sugars synthesized during daylight hours. Night warming of 1°C during grain filling reduces rice yields by approximately 10%.
